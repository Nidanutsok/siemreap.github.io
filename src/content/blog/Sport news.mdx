Title = Breaking Into Sport: The Cambodian Woman Balancing Vovinam, Motherhood and Business

Walking into the training ground with two of her sons—one in her arms, another following from behind—is Soeur Chanleakna, a 31-year-old Vovinam martial artist and double gold medalist at the 2023 SEA Games.

She takes a seat on a chair at the National Sports Training Centre where she talks about her passion and challenges in her 16-year sports career.

A wife to a boxing coach and mother to a five-year-old and two-year-old, Chanleakna told Kiripost that she won a gold and silver in the 8th World Vovinam Championship in Bali, Indonesia in 2025.

She first started Vovinam , a traditional Vietnamese martial art, to learn self defence then fell in love with the sport, prompting her to turn it into a career. “Even though I had a baby, I still love the sport.” 

It also helped her to recover her body after giving birth, boosting her physical and mental health. She said after delivery, she felt that her body and mind were not the same as before. People also made comments about her, which pushed her to train to strengthen herself. 

“After giving birth to my sons, I was deeply depressed. I feared I was not like before, afraid of people saying that women lose their beauty and strength after childbirth and cannot do anything except stay home and look after kids.” 

However, Chanleakna proved them wrong. She knew she was overthinking things and managed to overcome that by continuing to train.

She did face challenges, particularly overlapping training days as competition neared, leading to low strength during the competition which was a big challenge for her.

These days, Chanleakna said despite not being a national athlete, she continues to attend training sessions.

She receives some financial support from the federation, she said, adding that financial difficulties have long been a challenge for her, particularly as she must also manage the cost of travelling and stay committed to her training. In addition, she needs to manage her time given her obligation as a mother.

However, Chanleakna showed that time cannot stop her dream, and is now selling water bottles. Running an online business has allowed her to continue training without much problem.

Recalling another difficult situation, she said she struggled to prepare for the SEA Games in 2023 at Morodok Techo Stadium as athletes required to train in quarantine during the Covid-19 pandemic.

This made her feel upset and homesick as could not go anywhere but the moment allowed Chanleakna and her team to get to know each other better and unite as they trained side by side everyday.

“As I compete in a team category, it requires a lot of understanding between teammates,” she said, adding that the federation understood her situation and 
allowed her to train only four times per week.

At present, she has received a chance from the Vovinam Federation to represent Cambodia again in the upcoming competition which is The 6th Asian Championship this late November 2026.

“As an athlete and a mother, I want to encourage all mothers out there, please don’t give up on yourself and your dream. Take part in a sport as it will make you stronger,” she said, urging them not to believe the things that people say. “Love yourself, take care of your health, and be happy.”

When asked what she plans for her future, she said: “I will try my best to become a coach.”

Another Vovinam sportswoman, Ou Solida, 16 years old  said she became involved in the sport when she was 11years old, noting that time management was the most challenging part for her at the time due to school.

From November 24 to 29, Solida will represent Cambodia in the junior category in the 6th Asian Vovinam Championship. 

Meanwhile, for retired athlete Ou Ratana, President of the Federation of Cambodia Vovinam, said that the federation always supports former national athletes by giving them a chance to become a coach and referee.

He said Vovinam has become popular after the 32nd SEA Games, adding that the sport came from Hanoi, Vietnam, where it was founded in 1938 by martial artist Nguyen Loc. Vovinam first made its way to Cambodia in 2008 under former federation president Roth Sokhorn’s patronage. He held the post till 2020.

Vovinam was brought here to expand the sports field. “Sports do not discriminate based on social class, race, or political affiliation. It is an ambassador of peace,” Ratana said.
